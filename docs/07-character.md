# Character — the visual language (O1, in progress)

Explored in `lab/character.html` against Polaris, Nucleo UI and Central Icons
(`node tools/refs.mjs` builds the licensed references locally; they are gitignored).

## How to read this table (C0)

**The intent is always balance, refined symbols, consistency, character.** That is what the set
is for. Every row below is a means to it — a number that was found to serve it once, written
down so it does not have to be rediscovered. **None of them outranks it.** When a rule and the
intent disagree, the intent is right and the rule has been carried outside its range.

So these decisions exist to hold the language together, not to constrain the next drawing.
Before applying any row below to an icon being constructed, ask the question in this order:

1. Does the rule, *here*, make this icon better and more of a piece with the set?
2. Or does it force a drawing the eye rejects — a hollow glyph, a dash where text belongs,
   a detail cut to a speck?

If (2), the rule is out of its range, and the answer is to say so with the measurement and put
it to the user — **not** to ship the worse drawing because the table said so, and not to bend
the rule silently. A rule that starts producing discrepancy is reporting a gap in itself.

This is not a licence to improvise: the range is widened once, in writing, with the numbers
that showed it, and the widening is scoped to what was measured — see C24 for what happens when
one icon's arithmetic is promoted to a corollary. The table already carries three cases of this
working as intended: **C19** (§6's one-stroke floor was written for line marks and runs; inside a
glyph it left a hollow middle, so the glyph gap was carved out and floored at 1), **C22** (the
`trash` tab legs trip lint at 20 and 16 where the strokes never share space, so the drawing
stands and the fix is owed to `tools/lint.mjs`), and **C23** (`file`'s fold and chamfer enclose a
counter, not a pair of strokes; measured as a pair the rule demanded a chamfer 65% of the body).

| C15 | **How an optical shift is decided.** (1) **Measure, don't copy.** Each shape gets its own recommendation from a stated measure; a value is never carried over from another icon. For single-bend marks the measure is the **end triangle**: the triangle through both ends and the bend's apex. The mark moves against that triangle's offset from the box centre, by **K = 0.5** of it (`tools/optical-sheet.mjs` computes it). **Ink or line mass is not the measure**: the chevrons' mass sat ~0 off centre, yet they needed 0.5. (2) **Each shape is judged on its own range.** Rows sit around its own recommendation (default ± 0.1 on each axis), plus the box centre and the shipped position; moves in either direction are allowed. There is no fixed grid shared by all shapes. (3) **Always on the optical sheet.** `node tools/optical-sheet.mjs <shapes…>`, viewed on `/lab/optical-shift.html`: a plain page with, per shape, one row per position, showing the mark in its canvas outline at 24 · 20 · 16, a 24 menu row, inline text, 40 square and circle buttons, and 32 · 28 buttons. The shipped row and the recommended row are tagged. Never a chat widget or the Construction board. (4) **The eye decides.** The measure is a starting point. The pick is made by eye at default 24, and the other sizes and masters scale by ink height (C14). (5) **Optical balance is judged in the canvas, against all four edges at once.** A mark must read *optically balanced* — not geometrically even — inside its square canvas (24 · 20 · 16) from the top, bottom, left and right together. **Which axes need balancing is decided per icon, from its shape:** a mark that mirrors onto itself left–right (chevron-down, weighted equally on both sides) is judged only up–down; one that mirrors top–bottom (a play triangle) only left–right; one lopsided both ways (the checks) on both, together, as a grid; one that mirrors both ways (plus, x, minus) is balanced by construction. `optical-sheet.mjs` detects the mirror symmetry and says which axes it judges (`--axes` overrides). **An axis already picked is locked:** a later sheet varies only the remaining axis (`--axes x` after the up–down pick) and keeps the locked shift in every row, the box-centre row included. The sheet varies the judged axes around the recommendation (±0.1 ±0.2 for one axis, a ±0.1 grid for two), plus the box centre and the shipped position, and shows each position **inside its canvas outline** at every master beside the UI contexts. Left–right scales by ink width, up–down by ink height. | User direction 2026-09-17, after a copied 0.5 and a one-grid-for-all sheet were caught, and: optical balance means balanced in the canvas from top, bottom, left and right together — "not evenly, optically balanced"; and: choose the axes per icon — chevron only top–bottom, play only left–right, some icons both.
